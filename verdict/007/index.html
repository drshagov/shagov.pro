VERDICT  007  Peptides

BPC-157. Can it heal injured tendons faster?

A scan confirms a torn tendon. Rehabilitation lies ahead. Then comes an offer: a course of BPC-157, the “Wolverine peptide”, to heal faster. It is banned in sport, has been discussed by FDA advisers and is praised by public figures. Is there evidence that it speeds up healing of injured tendons in people?

Verdict issued and published 26 Nov 2026 Evidence last reviewed 16 Nov 2026
VerdictWhat to doWhat it is13 daysEvidenceSources
SHAGOV  |  VERDICT.Not justified.Issued · 26.11.2026

BPC-157 has not been shown to speed up healing of injured tendons in people. On current evidence, routine commercial use for this purpose is not justified. This is not a finding that BPC-157 is ineffective.

Banned in sport is not the same as proven to work.

Promise checked
“BPC-157 helps injured tendons heal faster.” Most often sold as a course of injections under the skin, alone or in “Wolverine” blends with another peptide, TB-500.
Checked for
Adults with a confirmed partial tendon tear, treated without surgery, using BPC-157 on its own. Separately: what is known for people aged 35–50. “Faster healing” means two results together: the tendon repairs faster, and function returns sooner, compared with the same rehabilitation without BPC-157.
What this verdict does not mean

That BPC-157 has been proven not to work. No study of adequate size shows that either. Its effect on injured human tendons is unknown.

That the animal research is worthless. Several rat studies reported faster tendon repair. They are shown in full below, with their limits.

That people who felt better are wrong about how they feel. Less pain is real to the person. It is not the same result as a healed tendon.

That physicians who offer it are acting in bad faith. This review judges a claim, not people.

Not justified does not mean proven impossible. This verdict concerns one specific promise for one group. It is dated and will be reconsidered when stronger evidence becomes available.

Take this with you

What to do

First, plainly: on current evidence, paying for BPC-157 to make an injured tendon heal faster is not justified.

What follows is practical. It is not a protocol for using BPC-157, and it is not a recommendation to buy it.

Less pain is not a stronger tendon.

  1. Get the diagnosis first.A partial tear, a full traumatic tear and chronic tendinopathy are different conditions with different treatment. If an injury is followed by sudden, marked weakness of an arm or a leg, see an orthopaedic specialist promptly. Don’t put a treatment decision on hold for an experimental course. (Lara et al., 2026)
  2. Judge recovery by tissue and function, not by pain alone.Pain can ease before a tendon is able to take load again. And a better-looking scan does not mean function has returned. Both results are needed.
  3. If you compete, it is banned.BPC-157 is prohibited by WADA at all times, in and out of competition. US regulatory decisions don’t change that. (WADA, 2026)
Three questions before you pay

What exactly is this, and how will you show that my tendon, not just my pain, has recovered faster?

  1. What exactly are you offering?BPC-157 alone, or in a blend with other peptides? Which form, which manufacturer, what proof of quality for this batch, and what legal status does the product have in this country?
  2. Where is the human study?Not in rats, and not on knee pain: a controlled study in people with this kind of tendon injury.
  3. How will you check the result?What will be measured, the tissue and the function, when, and compared with what?
01 · What It Is

A lab-made peptide. Not an approved medicine.

Peptides are short chains of amino acids. Some are well-studied medicines, such as insulin and semaglutide. BPC-157 is a synthetic peptide of 15 amino acids (GEPPPGKPADDAGLV).

Belonging to the same class of molecules says nothing about a specific product. No government regulator has approved BPC-157 as a medicine. In its 2026 review, the FDA found no approved BPC-157 product in any country, and no centrally authorised product was found in the European Union. That alone does not mean it is banned everywhere: national rules differ. (FDA, 2026; EMA)

How it behaves in the body has been studied in rats and dogs. Reliable data on how much reaches the blood and tissues after commercial injections in people are lacking. (He et al., 2022; FDA, 2026)

Research papers link its sequence to a “body protection compound” described in gastric juice. That does not show that the human body makes an active BPC-157 drug, and it does not make an injection a natural treatment. Most of the early research comes from one group in Zagreb, Croatia, which has studied the molecule since the 1990s.

Two forms exist, free base and acetate. They are different pharmaceutical substances, and products sold under the same name can differ in purity and content. (FDA, 2026; HHS, 2026)

02 · The Promise

From rat studies. To a price list.

BPC-157 is sold for soft-tissue injuries, including tendons and ligaments. The typical claim: it “supports” or “accelerates” repair. The stronger version: the tissue heals faster and you return to load sooner.

It is sold through physician-led programs, telehealth services and online shops that label vials “for research use only”. BPC-157 on its own and blends with TB-500 are different interventions, and results for one cannot be transferred to the other.

Podcasters and athletes describe faster recovery after injuries. These stories can be entirely sincere. But tendons also heal on their own, with time and rehabilitation. Without a comparison with people who did not receive the peptide, no one can say that BPC-157 made the difference.

What it promises, and when

Sellers often add less pain and better mobility within one to four weeks. These timelines describe how people feel. They are not a measured timeline of tendon repair, which for a partial tear usually takes longer. This review does not treat them as the promise.

What it costs

Courses usually run four to eight weeks. In Dubai, a 10 mg injection pen was advertised at about AED 1,650 and a single clinic session at about AED 1,199. In the US, offers range from monthly subscriptions of a few hundred dollars to single vials. “Research use only” shops in Europe sell 5 mg vials from about €19. Prices change; this is an illustration, not a market index. Sellers are not named: the verdict concerns the claim, not the seller.

From rat to patient. Where it breaks.

04 · Banned In Sport

Banned in sport. So it works?

The most persuasive signal is also the most misread.

The WADA list

A ban can mean “unapproved”, not “effective”.

Testosterone, growth hormoneBy action

Listed in their own classes, S1 and S2, because of what they do in the body.

BPC-157Class S0

The class for substances with no current approval by any government health authority for human therapeutic use. Prohibited at all times. (WADA, 2026)

Banned ≠ proven.

Anti-doping rules aim to protect athletes and fair competition. They often act before the evidence is complete. A substance in class S0 is banned because nobody has approved it, not because it has been shown to work.

05 · Why Now

13 days. Three developments. No new proof of tendon healing.

In 2026, BPC-157 moved from grey-market shelves onto regulators’ agendas. The clinical question did not move with it.

  • July 2026: the FDA’s Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee voted 8–6, with one abstention, to recommend adding BPC-157 to the list of substances pharmacies may compound. FDA staff had recommended against it. (FDA, 2026; RAPS, 2026)
  • 28 October 2026, published 30 October: HHS said it would not take enforcement action against pharmacy compounding of BPC-157 acetate for an individual patient, if ten conditions are met, including a single active ingredient. Blends with TB-500 do not qualify. (HHS, 2026)
  • 9 November 2026: Hims & Hers said it would later expand into compounded BPC-157 and TB-500 and manufacture them in the US. (Reuters, 2026)
  • 5 and 10 November 2026: a UAE federal circular on peptide products listed BPC-157 acetate. The Dubai Health Authority then required facilities to stop using, prescribing and dispensing covered products immediately and to quarantine them, with an inventory due by 5 December. (EDE, 2026; DHA, 2026)
  • 12 November 2026: Health Canada warned about unauthorised injectable peptides seized from one company and reported bacteria in one product. BPC-157 was among the seized products; the regulator did not say which vial was contaminated. (Health Canada, 2026)

Regulatory decisions can change in days. Clinical evidence takes years.

The FDA vote

Advisers voted on compounding. Not on tendons.

What was reviewedColitis

The committee reviewed BPC-157 free base and acetate for ulcerative colitis. The vote is advisory; the FDA has not adopted it as a rule.

What was not evaluatedTendons

Tendinitis was named in the nomination, but the FDA could not evaluate it: no clinical studies were found. (FDA, 2026)

A vote on access ≠ proof of healing.

A policy change, a celebrity story, a sport ban and a positive rat experiment are different kinds of information. None of them shows the promised result in people.

The verdict is not borrowed from a regulator. It follows from the human evidence, the outcome that is promised and the price of error.

Evidence at a glance

What was promised, and what was shown.

Implied promise
A course of BPC-157 makes an injured tendon heal faster, and you get back to load sooner.
Proof needed
A randomised, placebo-controlled study in adults with a confirmed partial tear, with the same rehabilitation in both groups and blinded assessment, showing both faster repair on ultrasound or MRI and an earlier, clinically meaningful return of function. Less pain alone would not be enough.
Evidence
Positive but inconsistent results in rats, mostly from one research group. Three small uncontrolled human studies, none in tendons. No controlled human data on tendon healing.
Trade-offs
Repeated injections, unknown long-term safety, uncertain product quality, money, and above all weeks of delay with a serious injury.
The detail

Four questions. Open any one.

01

What is promised, and to whom?

Faster tendon repair and a quicker return to load. Sold to adults after injuries, often as a course of several weeks.

The promise, as checked: a course of BPC-157 makes a confirmed partial tendon tear heal faster in an adult treated without surgery.

How the promise sounds

Narrow versions say it “may support” healing of tendons and ligaments. Stronger versions say it “accelerates healing” of tendons, ligaments and joint tissue. Some add relief of pain and better mobility within one to four weeks. The promise also expands as it is sold: from recovery to “anti-aging”.

Who was actually studied

Knee pain16 of 17 followed upMixed knee conditions; injections into the joint; 12 received BPC-157 alone, 4 a blend with thymosin beta-4. (Lee & Padgett, 2021)Not a tendon tear. Retrospective, no control.
Bladder condition12 womenInterstitial cystitis; a single injection into the bladder wall. (Lee et al., 2024)Not related to tendons.
Intravenous test2 adultsTwo infusions over two days. (Lee & Burgess, 2025)Tolerability only.
Ulcerative colitis53 randomisedRectal BPC-157 for 14 days; reported only as a conference abstract. (FDA, 2026)Not related to tendons.
Health records1,039 usersUS clinical notes, 2020–2025; more than half also used other agents. (Biology Methods and Protocols, 2026)Describes use, not effect.
What this means

The promise is made to adults with tendon injuries. No published human study enrolled them. In the health-record sample, users averaged about 50 years of age: that describes demand, not effect. Ages 35–50: no direct evidence.

02

How could faster healing be shown?

Two results together: tissue that repairs faster on a blinded scan, and function that returns sooner. Pain is secondary.

“It healed faster” can mean three different things: the pain eased, the scan looks better, or the tendon can take load again. They are often merged into one.

Link one: the tissue

A validated ultrasound or MRI protocol, done at the same time points in both groups and read by assessors who do not know who received what. Measured: the size of the defect, continuity of the fibres, signs of a new tear. A normalised MRI signal on its own does not mean the patient has recovered.

Link two: the function

A validated function score for that tendon (for the shoulder, for example, SPADI or ASES), objective strength and load capacity, and a return to work or sport defined in advance.

Why less pain is not enough

Pain can ease before a tendon regains its strength. A person who feels better may return to load too early. Pain matters, but here it is a secondary result.

Why timing matters

Follow-up should match the natural course of the specific injury, not the one to four weeks named in advertising.

What this means

Both links can be measured with today’s tools. No human study of BPC-157 has measured either of them in a tendon injury.

03

What has been shown?

Positive but inconsistent signals in rats and cells. In people: small uncontrolled studies of other conditions.

There is a real experimental signal, and we show it in full. What it does not show is faster healing of a human tendon.

Rats, 2003–2006 · one research group

Staresinic et al., 2003Cut Achilles tendonDaily injections into the abdomen. Higher strength, better gait and collagen organisation within 14 days. (Staresinic et al., 2003)Rodent, surgical injury, short follow-up; not a course under the skin in people.
Krivic et al., 2006Tendon detached from bonePositive changes in structure, strength and function by day 21. (Krivic et al., 2006)A tendon-to-bone model, not a partial tear.

Rats, 2026 · independent group

Four tendons per group were tested for strength, at a single time point. The numbers lean towards BPC-157, but the study could not confirm the difference. This is not proof that BPC-157 does not work. It is not confirmation either. (Biçer et al., 2026)

Cells · independent group

In tendon cells and tendon explants, BPC-157 increased cell migration and survival; cell proliferation itself did not increase under the conditions tested. This supports a mechanism. It says nothing about the strength of a human tendon. (Chang et al., 2011)

People · what the studies reported

Knee painLee & Padgett, 202111 of 12 on BPC-157 alone reported less pain. Patients paid for the treatment and were surveyed afterwards; no control, imaging or function test.
Bladder conditionLee et al., 2024All reported improvement in symptoms. No control.
Intravenous testLee & Burgess, 2025No reactions recorded in short follow-up. Two people cannot establish safety.
Ulcerative colitisAbstract, 2005The difference between groups was not convincing. Not published in full.
Health records2026279 of 354 users with a recorded direction reported improvement; 685 had no assessment. No comparison; effect and safety cannot be inferred.

Registered trials

A University of Arkansas pilot plans to give BPC-157 under the skin for 90 days after arthroscopic repair of a full rotator cuff tear. As of 16 November 2026, it was not yet recruiting. Even if completed, it would test recovery after surgery, not a partial tear treated without it. (NCT07803250) An earlier safety study from a company in the BPC-157 field has had an unknown status since 2015, with no results posted. (NCT02637284)

The limits of this review. This is a structured critical audit of the sources, read in full text where available, not a new systematic review. Evidence from people, animals and cells is kept separate. Two systematic reviews were used as a cross-check. (Vasireddi et al., 2025; Poolton et al., 2026) Where only an abstract or a registry record exists, it is marked. Not finding a study in the sources checked is not proof that none exists anywhere. Outside the scope: chronic tendinopathy without a tear, full tears, recovery after surgery, ligaments, muscles, other uses of BPC-157 and blends with TB-500.
What this means

Four statements, kept apart. A signal in rats: shown, with limited independent replication. Faster repair of a human tendon: not studied. Earlier return of function: not studied. Long-term safety: not established.

A signal in rats. A question in people.

Evidence quality: very low for indirect effects on symptoms; for faster tendon healing in people, effectively absent. Ages 35–50: no direct evidence.

04

What does a mistake cost?

Weeks of delay with a serious injury, unknown long-term safety, uncertain product quality, and money.

With a confirmed tendon tear, the main risk may not be the peptide. It may be the time.

Most safety data come from animals and from reports that cannot establish cause. Rates of harm in people are unknown in either direction.

0controlled human studies of the safety of a tendon course
10serious adverse event reports to the FDA since 2021, eight of them in 2026, as reported by Reuters; cause and frequency cannot be established from them (Reuters, 2026)
4–8 wka typical advertised course: weeks in which a decision about the injury can be put off

1. Lost time

The cost that is easiest to miss is a delayed diagnosis of a worsening tear, delayed rehabilitation or a late decision about surgery. For traumatic rotator cuff tears, a 2026 review by shoulder surgeons recommends deciding about repair early, while noting that the evidence on timing is of very low certainty and that surgery is not right for everyone. (Lara et al., 2026) If the pain eases during a course, it is easy to believe the tendon has healed when it has not regained its strength.

2. The substance

The FDA noted changes in blood-clotting and liver measures in rats and dogs after 28 days of injections into muscle. Data on long-term use and cancer risk are lacking. These are signals and gaps, not proven complications in people. (FDA, 2026)

3. The injections

Repeated injections under the skin or into muscle carry general risks of infection and local complications. Two people who tolerated two infusions cannot settle that. (Lee & Burgess, 2025)

4. The product

Identity, form, sterility, endotoxins, dose accuracy and impurities vary by source. Health Canada found bacteria in one seized injectable peptide product from a company whose stock included BPC-157. (Health Canada, 2026)

5. Sport

For athletes under WADA rules, any use is a violation, whatever the form or stated purpose. (WADA, 2026)

What this means

These risks are different. A lack of safety data is not proof of harm, and one contaminated product does not mean every product is contaminated. But together they matter when the benefit has not been shown.

The main cost is not a side effect. It is time with a serious injury, spent waiting for a result that has not been shown.

Money comes back. Time doesn’t always.

Who paid for the evidence?

Funders, materials and ties of the key studies, named on both sides.

Much of the early positive animal data comes from one research group in Zagreb. That does not make it wrong. It is a reason to look for independent replication.

Patent. US patent US6288028B1, on BPC peptide salts and their use in therapy, lists among its inventors Predrag Sikirić and Sven Seiwerth, leading authors of the preclinical work. Filed with priority in 1997, granted in 2001, now expired. It documents a link between leading researchers and intellectual property on BPC-157 derivatives. It does not show current payments, income during specific experiments, or that their results are unreliable.

Staresinic et al., 2003. Supported by the Ministry of Science of Croatia; the paper mentions the pharmaceutical company PLIVA in the context of development. A direct payment from a manufacturer for this experiment was not established.

Krivic et al., 2006. Listed as non-US government research support. The specific grant and the maker of the test batch were not established.

Chang et al., 2011. Chang Gung University, Taiwan. The manufacturer and full funding were not established.

Lee & Padgett, 2021. The peptide was compounded by Tailor Made Compounding, Kentucky. Each patient paid for the peptide and the injection; the author is linked to the clinic where they were treated.

Lee et al., 2024. An unnamed compounding pharmacy. The authors declare no conflicts of interest.

Biçer et al., 2026. No external funding and no declared conflicts. The peptides came from Peptide Sciences, Florida; supplying a product is not sponsorship.

Vasireddi et al., 2025. No funding for the review. Some authors declared ties with orthopaedic companies (Stryker, Smith & Nephew, Arthrex, Mitek); this is not evidence of an interest in BPC-157.

Poolton et al., 2026. Funding and conflicts of interest could not be established from the available text.

We apply the same scrutiny to studies that support our verdict and those that challenge it. A financial relationship is not evidence that a finding is false. Nor does declaring a conflict resolve every question about independence.

SHAGOV  |  VERDICT.Not justified.Issued · 26.11.2026

BPC-157 has not been shown to speed up healing of injured tendons in people. On current evidence, routine commercial use for this purpose is not justified. This is not a finding that BPC-157 is ineffective.

BPC-157 may have a scientific future. But a possible future is not a proven result.

Evidence reviewed: 16 November 2026. The verdict will be reconsidered if convincing new evidence emerges: for example, a randomised controlled trial in adults with a confirmed partial tendon tear treated without surgery, showing both faster repair of the tissue and an earlier return of function. Trials after surgery or in other tissues may be useful, but would not on their own answer the question asked here.

Disclosure: I have never prescribed BPC-157, trained physicians in its use, or received financial compensation for BPC-157-related activities.

This VERDICT evaluates the commercial promise that BPC-157 speeds up healing of injured tendons in adults. It does not evaluate other uses of BPC-157, other peptides, or blends of BPC-157 with TB-500. No clinic or product is recommended.
